# problem: 
there is an infinite line of charge with uniform charge density lam on the y axis. we have a charged mass
q = Charge(q, m) with initial position [D, 0, 0] and 0 initial velocity

There is a wire with a perfectly circluar loop (radius R) that has a center at [2D, 0, R] that constrains the path of the q. with no friction. we can assume the force is great enough for the q to traverse the whole loop. There is no gravity in the problem. 

Find the magnitude of the normal force at the very top of the circular loop. 


# soln 
this problem requires first find E(r) for infinite wire with quick gauss law (easy)

E(r) = 2 k lam rhat / r

then we need to apply F = m a and specifically the bead at the top of the loop is not stationary, there is a net downward acceleration on it, meaning that the electric force pushing up is less than the normal force of the wire. 

we have to use a fact about circular motion that the magnitude of the acceleration is v2/r 

since we are at the very top the direction of this acceleration (normal) is -jhat

so F = ma 
Fe + Fn = m * v2/R

The electric force can be found by first drawing a right triangle between the top of the loop the x axis and the wire at the origin. then find the displacement.

r = sqrt((2d)2 + (2R)2) = 2 sqrt(d2 + r2)

we can make a theta between [2d, 0], origin, [2d, 2R]. then since Fe is radially directed we can multiply by sin theta to get the vertical jhat component

sin theta = 2R/(2 sqrt(d2 + R2))
    = R / (sqrt (d2 + R2 ))

E(P) = 2 k lam / r rhat
    = 2 k lam / (2 sqrt(d2 + r2))
    = k lam / sqrt(d2 + r2)

Ey(P) = E(P) * sin theta
    = k lam / sqrt(d2 + r2) * R / (sqrt (d2 + R2 ))
    = k lam R / (D2 + R2) jhat

Fe_y = q * Ey(P)
    = q k lam R / (D2 + R2) jhat

Fe_y + Fn = m v2/r   | Fn is purely in -jhat direction  

The problem doesn't provide v so we must find it with work energy theorem.

The way to find how much kinetic energy the bead has is to do a quick line integral to find the change in potential

W = Delta U  (sys = bead), no potential energy 
-q Delta V = Delta KE   | KEi = 0 by given. its -qDV because its the work done by the potential difference 
-q int(ri, rf) E dot dl = 1/2 m vf^2 

ri = D, rf = 2 sqrt(D2 + R2)

Delta V = - 2 k lam ln((2 sqrt(D2 + R2) / D))

so 2 q k lam ln((2 sqrt(D2 + R2) / D)) = 1/2 m vf2 

end up with 
vf = sqrt(4 q k lam / m * ln((2 sqrt(D2 + R2) / D)))

Fn_y = m v2 / R - Fe 
    = m * 4 q k lam / R m * ln((2 sqrt(D2 + R2) / D)) - q k lam R / (D2 + R2)
