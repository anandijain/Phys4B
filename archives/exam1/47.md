problem:

theta: angle between horizontal and rod
rod(charge=Q, len=L, pos=(origin, [L cos theta, L sin theta]))
m = (mass=m, pos= hanging from [L/2 cos theta, L/2 sin theta])

uniform E field = E ihat 

find the mass m such that the rod has no angular acceleration

# soln 

Tau = I alpha       | alpha is zero by problem

Tau = 0 = (r_tension cross F_tension) + (r_rod cross F_rod)

1) find the torque on the rod due to the hanging mass:

F = m a (sys = m). a must be zero since we are at equillibrium
forces: gravity and tension 
Fg + Ft = 0 
-mg jhat + Ft = 0
Ft = m g jhat 

This means that the force of the string tension on the rod = Fg = -m g jhat

so F(tension->rod) = -m g jhat
now the r_tension is the vector for the displacement from the pivot to the point of applied force
    = [L/2 cos theta, L/2 sin theta]

now we can evalutate cross product

det [
    i           j           k
    L/2 cos     L/2 sin     0
    0           -m g        0
]

= -khat L m g / 2 cos theta

2) now we need to find the torque due to the electric field
Tau_E = (r_rod cross F_rod)
    = r(s) cross Evec dq        | explicitly putting Evec because we can only take the cross product between 2 vectors. a scalar in a cross product makes no sense. 

find r(s) and dq, where s is the distance along the curve (integration variable)

r(s) = [s cos theta, s sin theta, 0]
dq/ds = Q/L = "lam"

dq = lam ds

dTau_E = [s cos theta, s sin theta, 0] cross Evec lam ds

so 

Tau_E = int(0, L) [s cos theta, s sin theta, 0] cross Evec lam ds

recall that Evec = E ihat
eval cross 
det [
    i           j           k
    s cos       s sin       0  
    E lam ds    0           0
]

= -khat E lam s sin theta ds 
Tau_E
    = int(0,L)-khat E lam s sin theta ds 
    = -khat E lam sin(theta) int(0, L) s ds => s2/2 @ (0, L) = L2/2
    = -khat E lam sin(theta) * L2 /2
    = -khat E Q sin(theta) / L * L2 / 2
    = -khat E Q sin(theta) L / 2

now for the torque balance 

Tau = 0 = Tau_E + Tau_T
    = -khat E Q sin(theta) L / 2 + -khat L m g / 2 cos theta        | scalarize and remove L/2
    = E Q sin(theta) + m g cos(theta)

m = - E Q sin(theta) / (g cos(theta))
    = - E Q tan(theta) / g

note that m is "negative" but that really just implies that E, Q, or g (or all 3) are negative since mass can't be negative.
